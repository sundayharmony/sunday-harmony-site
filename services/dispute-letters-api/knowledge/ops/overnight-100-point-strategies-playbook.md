# “~100 points overnight” strategies — study notes + ops checklist

**Source video:** [3 Ways to increase credit score 100 points over night!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=84Hi8hcQ3mw)  
**Creator:** Stephen Smith — *Success with Stephen*

> **Not legal advice / not financial or tax advice.** 401(k) loans and credit products have serious risks. Evaluate with qualified advisors.

---

## 1. Deep summary

### Goal
Show three **unconventional, reporting-cycle** moves that can change multiple score factors at once so a jump *looks* overnight after bureaus update — not “pay on time / don’t apply / keep balances low” basics.

### Framing
- Scores can sit flat then jump when several inputs change in one reporting cycle.
- Bigger upside on thin / young / high-utilization profiles; less drama if already ~780+.
- Derogatories (lates, collections, charge-offs, inaccurate negatives) can **cap** gains — dispute/clean those too (points to Rising).

### Three strategies
1. **Credit age / depth via retroactive rent & utility reporting** (back ~24 months when eligible).
2. **Move revolving utilization off the consumer report** without adding a consumer installment tradeline — spotlight: **401(k) plan loan** (also mentions BHG-style non-reporting personal loan / business credit videos).
3. **Credit limit transplant** — reallocate limit between cards at the **same issuer** to fix a single maxed card’s utilization without new inquiry / new account.

Stacking all three (plus cleaning derogatories) is how “100-point” stories happen.

## 2. Beat-by-beat detail

### 2.1 Strategy 1 — Make old on-time rent/utility history visible
- Length of history ≈ **15%** of FICO; milestones (esp. ~**2-year** average age) can unlock jumps.
- Use a service that can **verify past** payments (not only future) and report to **all three** bureaus when possible.
- Rising recommended for eligible rent/utility back-reporting.
- Requirements: real on-time history in bank; service setup correct.
- Newer models (FICO 9 / 10 family, VantageScore 4.0) weigh rental more; many lenders still on older FICO 8 — results vary.
- Example: thin 590 file + 2 years verified rent/utilities adds age, depth, recent positives at once.

### 2.2 Strategy 2 — Remove reported revolving utilization without “paying off” in the FICO sense
- Replace bureau-reported card debt with debt that **doesn’t create a consumer revolving/installment tradeline**.
- **401(k) loan angle:**
  - Large share of adults have retirement accounts.
  - Often no consumer credit approval; typically **does not** report as consumer installment.
  - Use proceeds to pay cards → reported revolving utilization collapses (e.g. 90% → ~2%).
  - Still owe the plan; interest/cost differs from 25–30% card APR.
  - **Risks he stresses:** plan must allow loans; federal max generally lesser of $50k or 50% vested (special small-balance rules); usually ≤5 years unless primary residence; opportunity cost / missed market growth; default → taxable distribution + possible penalty; job change can complicate repayment.
  - Evaluate as **financial decision first**, credit tactic second.
- Also points to prior videos: non-reporting personal loan (BHG), business credit to park debt off personal profile.

### 2.3 Strategy 3 — Credit limit transplant (same bank)
- FICO cares about **overall** and **per-card** utilization / number of accounts with balances.
- Ask issuer to move unused limit from Card B → Card A so a 90% card becomes e.g. 36% without paying down, new account, or (usually) hard pull.
- Not all banks allow; rules vary (CSR vs credit/lending review vs reconsideration).
- Stronger when combined with paydowns; can later move limit back.
- Example math with $10k@90% + $20k@0 → after transplant $25k@36% + $5k@0; totals unchanged.

### 2.4 Stacking example
580 score, 9-month file, maxed cards → back-report rent/utilities + plan loan crush utilization + transplant fix individual maxed card → one reporting cycle rewrites age, payment depth, revolving risk.

### 2.5 Limits
Works best when utilization / thin file / single maxed cards are the main problem.  
Recent lates/collections/charge-offs/duplicates/inaccurate balances remain anchors — identify and challenge those (Rising pitch).

## 4. MANUAL checklist

### Before any “overnight” tactic
- [ ] In product: pull/analyze report; note utilization, age, thin-file, derogs.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Fix/dispute inaccurate or unverifiable negatives that would cap gains.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Confirm whether profile is utilization-bound vs derogatory-bound.

### Strategy 1 — Rent/utility
- [ ] **MANUAL** Verify eligible on-time history exists.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Choose a service that back-reports and covers needed bureaus (e.g. Rising).
- [ ] **MANUAL** Complete bank connect / verification; monitor bureau posting.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Set expectations: model version & lender FICO version vary.

### Strategy 2 — 401(k) / non-reporting debt move
- [ ] **MANUAL** Confirm plan allows loans; read plan docs / HR / advisor.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Model repayment, taxes on default, job-change risk, opportunity cost.
- [ ] **MANUAL** If proceeding: pay cards, wait for statement/bureau update, re-check scores.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Alternative products (business cards, specialty loans) — qualification & reporting rules are client-specific.

### Strategy 3 — Limit transplant
- [ ] **MANUAL** Identify same-issuer card pairs.
- [ ] **MANUAL** Call issuer; document whether reallocation allowed and any credit review.
- [ ] **MANUAL** After limits update, verify per-card utilization on next report.
- [ ] Optional: combine with targeted paydown; optionally move limit back later.

### Product does not
1. Originate 401(k) loans or non-reporting consumer loans.
2. Call banks for limit transplants.
3. Enroll rent/utility furnishers.
4. Promise 100-point jumps.
5. Replace tax/retirement counseling.
