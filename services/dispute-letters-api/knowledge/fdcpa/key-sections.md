## §805 — Communication in Connection with Debt Collection (15 U.S.C. §1692c)

### Restrictions on contacting consumers (§805(a))

Without prior consent or court permission, a debt collector may not communicate with a consumer:

- At unusual times or places known (or that should be known) to be inconvenient (presumed convenient: 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time)
- If consumer is represented by an attorney (with exceptions)
- At the consumer's place of employment if employer prohibits such communication

### Third-party communications (§805(b))

May not communicate about the debt with third parties except as permitted (consumer, attorney, CRA, creditor, or court-authorized).

### Cease communication (§805(c))

If consumer notifies collector **in writing** that they refuse to pay or wish communication to cease, collector must stop except to:

1. Advise that efforts are terminated
2. Notify that specified remedies may be invoked
3. Notify intent to invoke a specified remedy

Written notice is complete upon receipt.

## §806 — Harassment or Abuse (15 U.S.C. §1692d)

Prohibited conduct includes:

- Violence or threats of violence
- Obscene or profane language
- Publishing lists of consumers who refuse to pay (with limited exceptions)
- Advertising debts for sale to coerce payment
- Repeated phone calls intended to annoy or harass
- Calls without meaningful disclosure of caller identity

---

## §807 — False or Misleading Representations (15 U.S.C. §1692e)

Prohibited representations include:

- False implication of government affiliation
- Misrepresenting character, amount, or legal status of debt
- False implication that individual is an attorney
- Threatening arrest, imprisonment, or property seizure not intended or not lawful
- Threatening action that cannot legally be taken
- **Failure to communicate that a disputed debt is disputed**
- Failing to disclose collector identity and debt-collection purpose in initial communications

---

## §808 — Unfair Practices (15 U.S.C. §1692f)

Prohibited practices include:

- Collecting amounts not authorized by agreement or law
- Accepting checks postdated more than 5 days unless consumer notified in writing
- Depositing postdated checks before the date
- Causing charges for communications (collect calls, etc.) by concealing purpose
- Taking or threatening nonjudicial property dispossession
- Communicating via postcard or using symbols on envelopes indicating debt collection

## §809 — Validation of Debts (15 U.S.C. §1692g)

### Initial notice (§809(a))

Within **5 days** after initial communication, collector must send written notice containing:

1. Amount of the debt
2. Name of the creditor
3. Statement that debt is assumed valid unless consumer disputes within **30 days**
4. Statement that if consumer disputes in writing within 30 days, collector will obtain verification and mail a copy
5. Statement that consumer may request original creditor name and address within 30 days

### Disputed debts (§809(b))

If consumer disputes in writing within 30 days (or requests original creditor info):

- Collector must **cease collection** of the debt or disputed portion until verification or original creditor information is mailed to consumer
- Collection may continue during the 30-day period only if consumer has not yet disputed in writing
- Communications during the 30-day period must not overshadow dispute rights

### No admission (§809(c))

Failure to dispute is not an admission of liability.

## §813 — Civil Liability (15 U.S.C. §1692k)

For failure to comply with any FDCPA provision:

- Actual damages
- Additional damages up to $1,000 per individual action (or class action limits)
- Costs and reasonable attorney's fees
- Bona fide error defense available if procedures reasonably adapted to avoid error
